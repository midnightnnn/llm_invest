Use `get_macro_research_briefing` when official macro research could reveal structural themes before they show up clearly in prices or rankings. It retrieves BOK and St. Louis Fed research summaries plus investable thesis seeds: transmission channels, affected sectors, candidate discovery queries, watch indicators, invalidation conditions, and caveats.

This is most useful when you want a forward-looking macro lens for portfolio decisions: climate transition, economic security, supply-chain restructuring, credit transmission, inflation persistence, labor shifts, financial stability, external balances, or policy regime changes. It does not recommend trades by itself; it gives research-backed hypotheses that can guide later discovery, risk checks, and portfolio construction.

Parameters:
- `scope`: one of `latest`, `week`, `month`, `quarter`, `all`; default `week`.
- `market`: `us`, `kr`, or `all`; default `all`.
- `sources`: optional list such as `["bok"]` or `["stlouisfed"]`.
- `source_doc_ids`: optional exact document ids returned by discovery calls. Use this for precise drilldown.
- `doc_types`: optional list such as `["issue_note"]`, `["working_paper"]`, `["research_report"]`, `["journal_article"]`, `["research_blog"]`.
- `themes`: optional macro tags such as `inflation`, `monetary_policy`, `rates`, `credit`, `growth`, `labor`, `financial_stability`, `external`.
- `detail_level`: `compact` for prompt-efficient context, `facts` for findings/methodology/channels/implications/watch indicators/caveats/investment theses, or `full` for stored metadata.
- `limit`: maximum rows to return, capped internally.

Use this when the decision needs official macro interpretation, central-bank research context, or a research-backed thesis seed for sector/industry discovery. Use `macro_snapshot` instead for numeric FRED/ECOS indicators and derived spreads.

Recommended flow: call broadly with `detail_level="compact"` first, inspect returned `source_doc_id` values, then call again with `source_doc_ids=[...]` and `detail_level="facts"` or `"full"` for exact document drilldown.
